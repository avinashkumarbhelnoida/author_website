Why Books?

Avinash Kumar

Someone once asked me, "Why books?"
My answer was simple: Why not?

Books are perhaps the best things that can happen to a human mind.

There are questions we are too afraid to ask aloud. Problems we cannot explain to the people around us. Thoughts that visit us at three in the morning and refuse to leave. Relationships we do not understand. Decisions we cannot make. Memories we cannot escape. Fears we cannot name. And sometimes, that strange feeling that everyone else seems to have figured life out—except us.

Then there is a book.

A good book does not always give you an answer. Sometimes, it gives you something more valuable: the right question.

It tells you that the confusion you thought was uniquely yours has been experienced by millions before you. That the feeling you could not put into words has a name. That the mistake you keep repeating has a pattern. That the person you cannot understand may be behaving according to a psychology you have never considered. That the past you thought you had left behind may still be quietly writing parts of your present.

Books become teachers when we need knowledge, companions when we need solitude, mirrors when we need to understand ourselves, and windows when we need to see a world larger than our own.

They can take us into minds we have never met, lives we have never lived, centuries we never witnessed, places we may never visit, and possibilities we never imagined.

A book can answer a question you have been carrying for years.

It can challenge an answer you have carried for even longer.

It can comfort you without knowing your name.

It can disagree with you without arguing.

It can change your mind without demanding that you surrender it.

And sometimes, a single sentence can arrive at precisely the right moment and rearrange something inside you.

That is the magic of books.

They do not merely fill the mind with information. They give the mind somewhere to go.

Perhaps that is why I write. Not because I believe every question has an answer, but because I believe every curious mind deserves a place to search.

Some books teach you about the world.

Some teach you about other people. And the rare ones make you turn the final page and realize that, somewhere along the way, you have been reading about yourself.

So, why books?

Why not?

After all, there are few things in life that can sit quietly on a shelf for years—and still be waiting for you when you finally become ready to hear what they have to say.
